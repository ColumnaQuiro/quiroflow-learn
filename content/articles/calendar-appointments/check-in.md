Checking a patient in tells the whole team they're in the waiting room: their visit becomes **Arrived** on the calendar and in the flow tracker, and their practitioner sees it on My Day. The front desk can do it, or the patient themselves on a tablet at the entrance or on their phone, so nobody has to get up whenever someone walks in.

## At the front desk

There are three places to do it, and all three do the same thing:

- in the appointment panel, the **Check in** button (see [Appointment stages and the appointment panel](/articles/calendar-appointments/appointment-statuses-explained));
- on [My Day](/articles/calendar-appointments/my-day), the arrival button on each row;
- and once checked in, the patient appears under **Arrived** in the [flow tracker](/articles/calendar-appointments/flow-tracker).

## Turn on the check-in portal (reception tablet)

::help-video{clip="checkin--enable-portal" caption="Turning on the clinic's check-in portal and copying the link for the tablet"}
::

The check-in portal is a screen for a tablet at the entrance, where the patient types their date of birth or DNI/NIE, or shows the QR code from their app, and is checked in. Their practitioner gets a notification.

1. Go to **Settings › Clinics**, open your clinic and scroll to **Check-in portal**. (You need the clinic configuration permission.)
2. Click **Turn on**.
3. Under **Patients identify themselves with**, choose **Date of birth** (six digits, DDMMYY) or **DNI / NIE** (harder to guess, but it can't find anyone without a DNI on their record).
4. Choose the **Tablet's language**. Patients can switch it on the tablet; it goes back to this one after each of them.
5. If you like, write your own wording for the **Welcome**, **Checked in** and **Appointment not found** screens. Anything left blank uses the standard text.
6. Click **Save**.
7. Under **Link for the tablet**, click **Copy** (or **Open**) and open that link on the tablet. Leave it there, plugged in.

The portal needs no login: the link is the key, so keep it to the tablet. If the tablet goes missing or the link is shared, click **New link…**: the old one stops working at once. **Turn off…** switches it off; turning it on again makes a new link, with the standard wording.

> **Good to know:** the portal only finds patients with a visit at that clinic starting within the next 90 minutes.

> **Good to know:** a USB or Bluetooth QR scanner plugged into the tablet works; without one, the tablet uses its own camera.

## What the patient sees on the tablet

::help-video{clip="checkin--kiosk" caption="A patient checks in on the tablet with their date of birth"}
::

1. The tablet shows a welcome ("Welcome! Check in here" in English) and a keypad for the date of birth (or the DNI/NIE and **Continue**). Below it, **Scan QR code** for anyone with the app.
2. The patient types their date, for example 070385 for 7 March 1985.
3. If two visits share that date of birth, it asks "Which one is you?" and shows each one's time.
4. Once found: "Hello, …!", "You are checked in. Take a seat, we will call you." and their visit time with their practitioner. **Done** returns to the start.

If it can't find the visit: "We could not find your appointment" with **Try again**. The screen clears itself after each patient.

## "I'm here" from the patient's phone

Patients with the QuiroFlow app or access to the patient portal can check themselves in: on the day of the visit, from 90 minutes before until it ends, they see an **I'm here** button. With nothing due in that window they see "There's no visit to check in to right now." The portal and app are covered in [The patient portal](/articles/patient-app/patient-portal).

## Signs to print

::help-video{clip="checkin--print-sign" caption="Opening the check-in sign to print it"}
::

In **Settings › Mobile App** there are two A4 signs for reception:

- **Print a check-in sign**: "Just arrived?" with a QR code. The patient scans it and taps "I'm here". Click **Print**.
- **Print a desk sign**: invites patients to download the app and join your clinic with the clinic's code. It doesn't check anyone in, but it usually goes next to the other one.

> **Good to know:** the check-in sign works for people with the app or portal access. For patients with neither, the check-in portal on the tablet is the better choice.
