**My Day** is the practitioner's screen: who's coming today, in what order, who has already arrived and, alongside, the visit itself for writing the note and signing it off. Below it sits what needs sorting before tomorrow: unconfirmed visits and tasks. It's in the sidebar under **Today** › **My Day**, with a number showing today's visits.

## What's on screen

- **Header:** **My Day**, with the day, how many visits there are and how many are charted. People who see all visits also get a picker to see another practitioner's day. On the right, **Privacy mode**.
- **Date:** ‹ **Today** › and the switch between **Day** and **Week**.
- **Clock card:** for clocking in and out (see [Working time](/articles/team-roles/working-time-log)). Once you've clocked in it shrinks to one line, such as "In 09:01 · working".
- **Stage strip:** how many visits are at each step: **Booked**, **Arrived**, **In room**, **Charted** (has a visit note) and **Invoiced** (marked done).
- **The day's list:** one row per visit, with the time, patient, type, a button to mark arrival, alerts (plan re-exam, red flags, pending forms), the step it's at and, where relevant, a risk pill: **No-show risk**, **Missed one** or **First visit**. The details button opens the same appointment panel as the calendar.

## See a patient and close the visit

::help-video{clip="myday--chart-and-sign" caption="Opening the patient who has arrived, writing the note and signing off the visit"}
::

1. When the patient arrives, click the arrival button on their row (**Mark as arrived**) if the front desk hasn't already.
2. Click their row. Their visit opens on the right: their attendance (**Completed**, **Cancelled**, **Missed**, **Show rate**) and the visit note. Opening it moves the visit to **In room**.
3. Write the note. **Copy last note** starts from the previous one; **Quick Note** is for something short. **Notes History**, **Files & Documents** and **View profile →** are to hand.
4. Click **Sign & complete**.

The visit is done, and in the stage strip it moves to **Invoiced**. How to write the note is covered in [Visit notes](/articles/clinical/visit-notes).

> **Good to know:** opening the row of a patient who has arrived moves them to **In session** automatically. There's no separate button for it.

> **Good to know:** **Sign & complete** marks the visit done but **doesn't charge anything**. "Invoiced" in the strip only means it's closed: payment is taken from the visit's **Billing** tab, on the calendar or in the details panel.

> **Good to know:** My Day's steps aren't the calendar's. The calendar follows the patient's journey (arrived, in session, to pay, done); My Day follows the clinical work (arrived, in room, note charted, closed).

## Needs confirming

::help-video{clip="myday--needs-confirming" caption="Confirming a visit by hand and messaging someone who asked to move theirs"}
::

The **Needs confirming** card gathers the visits from now until tomorrow night that nobody has confirmed, riskiest first. Patients who asked to move their visit (**Asked to move it**) come top.

1. Choose to see only yours (**Mine**) or **Everyone**'s.
2. Each row has **Call**, **Message** (opens the conversation in the inbox) and a button to mark it **Confirmed**.
3. If there are more than a few, **Show all N** lists them all.

When everything is confirmed, the card says "Everything until tomorrow night is confirmed." When there's nothing to confirm, it doesn't show.

## Tasks

**Tasks** lists what an automation has asked you or your role to do (with its notify step): the automation's name, when it's due and a box to **Mark as done**. Three show at a time; **Show all N** shows the rest. Automations are covered in [Automations](/articles/communications/automations).

Owners also see a low-stock warning, with **Receive a delivery**.

## Who sees it

**My Day** shows for anyone whose **Calendar** permission is **All appointments** or **Only their own**. With **All appointments**, the header shows the practitioner picker. Only practitioners appear: the front desk has no list of its own here. The clock card is always yours, even while you're looking at another practitioner's day.
