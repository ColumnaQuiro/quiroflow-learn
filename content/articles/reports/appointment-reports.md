Three reports under **Reports › Operations** about the schedule. **Upcoming Visits** shows how the month fills up, day by day. **Patient Flow** measures waits, delays and how long sessions last. **Appointment Distribution** compares mornings, afternoons and the last hours of the day by volume and attendance.

## Upcoming Visits

::help-video{clip="reports--upcoming-visits" caption="Upcoming Visits: this month and the next"}
::

1. Open **Reports › Upcoming Visits**. It opens on the current month.
2. Use the **Previous month** and **Next month** arrows to move around; **Today** takes you back to the current month.
3. Read the three figures and the two charts.

**What counts.**
- **Total this month**: every appointment in the month, whatever its status (booked, completed, cancelled, no-show).
- **vs. previous month**: how much that total is up or down compared with the previous month.
- **Daily average**: the total divided by all the days of the month, weekends and closed days included.
- **By day of month**: one bar per day, with the **Kept** appointments (booked or completed) and the **Cancelled / no-show** ones stacked on top.
- **By day of week**: how many appointments fall on each day, to see which days fill up first.

Use it to spot a slow week in good time and act (reminders, waiting list, advertising) before it arrives. This report has no practitioner or clinic filter; with **Only their own figures** it counts only your own appointments.

## Patient Flow

::help-video{clip="reports--patient-flow" caption="Waits, delays and how long sessions last"}
::

**Patient Flow** is built from the marks the front desk puts on the calendar when the patient arrives, goes in to the session and leaves (see [Calendar and appointments](/articles/calendar-appointments)). If they aren't marked, the report says "No checked-in visits in this period.".

1. Open **Reports › Patient Flow**, choose the period and, if you like, a practitioner or a clinic.
2. Read the cards at the top:
   - **Visits recorded**: visits with the arrival marked, and what percentage they are of completed visits. If the percentage is low, the averages tell you little.
   - **Average wait**: from arrival to going in with the practitioner (with the median underneath).
   - **Seen on time**: the percentage of visits where the patient went in with the practitioner less than 5 minutes after the appointment time.
   - **Average session**: from going in with the practitioner to checkout.
   - **Patients arrive**: the median of how early or late they arrive compared with their time ("on time", "N min early", "N min late").
3. Under **The visit, stage by stage**, look at **Waiting** (arrival → with the practitioner), **Late** (appointment time → with the practitioner) and **In session** (with the practitioner → checkout). The colour is green under 10 minutes, amber under 20 and red above that.
4. **How long, how often** spreads the visits across minute bands, and **By practitioner** gives each one's average wait, delay and session.

## Appointment Distribution

::help-video{clip="reports--appointment-distribution" caption="Which shift performs best and at what hours there are most appointments"}
::

1. Open **Reports › Appointment Distribution**, choose the period and, if you like, a practitioner or a clinic.
2. At the top there's a card for each shift: **Morning (before 12pm)**, **Afternoon (12–4pm)** and **Evening (4pm+)**, with their appointments and their attendance rate.
3. **By shift, by outcome** stacks, within each shift, the **Completed**, **No-show**, **Cancelled** and **Booked (upcoming)** appointments.
4. **By hour of day** counts the appointments in each hour between 8:00 and 20:00.

**The attendance rate** is completed divided by completed plus no-shows. Cancelled appointments don't count in it (the patient let you know) and neither do booked ones (they haven't happened yet). A shift with few no-shows has a high rate even if it has many cancellations.

> **Good to know:** the hours in **By hour of day** appear in 12-hour format (*8am*, *1pm*…). *1pm* is one in the afternoon, *4pm* is four.
