A care plan tracks a patient's planned course of care -- for example, "10 visits, every 2 weeks" -- and shows progress right on their Overview tab, so anyone opening the patient can see where they stand without digging through appointment history.

![Care Plan card showing "Wellness Plan, 1 of 10 visits, every 2 weeks" with a progress bar](/screenshots/patient-overview.png)

## Creating one

On the patient's **Overview** tab, find the **Care Plan** card and select **Add plan** (or **Edit** if one already exists).

Give it a name, the total number of planned visits, and how often they're expected (e.g. every 1 or 2 weeks). QuiroFlow tracks completed visits against the plan automatically as appointments are marked complete, showing a simple progress bar like "3 of 10 visits" -- there's nothing extra to log manually as the patient works through it.

## Why it's useful

A care plan is informational -- it doesn't block booking or billing, and a patient can still be booked for a visit outside the plan's expected cadence -- but it's a quick way for both staff and practitioners to see whether a patient is on track, falling behind, or due to wrap up their course of treatment. It's a good conversation-starter at the front desk too: a patient who's "3 of 10, every 2 weeks" but hasn't visited in a month is worth a quick check-in before they're formally overdue for a Recall.

## When a plan finishes

There's no automatic action when a patient completes all their planned visits -- QuiroFlow doesn't book a follow-up or send a message on its own. Treat a completed plan as a natural checkpoint to have that conversation directly with the patient (continue the same plan, start a new one, or wrap up care), and select **Edit** to set up whatever comes next.

## One plan at a time

A patient has a single active care plan at a time -- if their treatment approach changes partway through, edit the existing plan rather than trying to run two in parallel. If you need a historical record of a completed plan before starting a new one, note it in Visit notes first, since editing overwrites the current plan's details rather than archiving them.
