A patient's **Visit notes** tab is their clinical record: one entry per visit, each tied to a date, appointment type, and practitioner. It's separate from -- and easy to confuse with -- two other note-like fields in QuiroFlow, so it's worth being clear on the difference before diving in.

## Where visit notes come from

Visit notes are added from an appointment, not typed directly into the Visit notes tab -- a brand-new patient with no completed visits shows "No visit notes yet -- these get added from an appointment." As a visit happens and is worked through (checked in, seen, marked complete), the note for that visit is what ends up listed here, stamped with the date, the appointment type, and which practitioner saw them.

Once written, each entry appears on the Visit notes tab in date order, most recent first -- practitioners scanning a familiar patient's history can see exactly what was noted last time before the patient even sits down.

## The three different "notes" in QuiroFlow

It's easy to reach for the wrong one, since all three sit close together in the UI:

- **Visit notes** (this article) -- clinical, one per visit, permanent part of the patient's history.
- **Patient note** -- a single persistent free-text field on the patient's Overview tab, for things that are true about the patient generally rather than tied to one visit: intake history, ongoing precautions, context a new practitioner should read before a first session. See [A patient's record](/articles/patients/patient-records).
- **Appointment note** -- the sticky note on the **Notes** tab of a specific appointment's editor (see [Booking an appointment](/articles/calendar-appointments/booking-an-appointment)), meant as a short-lived reminder for whoever handles that one appointment rather than a clinical record.

A practical rule: if it should still matter a year from now as part of this patient's treatment history, it's a visit note. If it's background that's always relevant, it's the Patient note. If it's only relevant until this one appointment is done, it's the appointment's own sticky note.

## Editing and deleting

Visit notes can be edited or removed by staff with the right permissions (see [Roles & permissions](/articles/team-roles/roles-and-permissions) for the specific clinical-note permissions a custom role can grant or withhold) -- useful for correcting a mistake, but treat it as a clinical record rather than a scratch pad, since it's what a practitioner picking up this patient later will actually rely on.
