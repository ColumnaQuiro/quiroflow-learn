The record's **Progress** tab shows at a glance whether the patient is getting better: how their pain has changed since they started, visit by visit, and their posture assessments. It's what you want in front of you at a re-exam, and with **Progress report** you can give it to them as a PDF.

::

## Where the pain scores come from

Each visit can have a pain score from 0 to 10. It's recorded in two ways:

- **By the practitioner**, when writing the note in **My Day**, with **Pain today**. See [Visit notes](/articles/clinical/visit-notes).
- **By the patient**, when checking in from their app or portal: after "I'm here" they're asked how much pain they have today. The practitioner sees it in **My Day** ("The patient gave N at check-in.").

## What the tab shows

- At the top: **Pain now**, **When they started**, **Change** and how many visits have a pain score.
- The **Pain by visit** chart, with a summary such as "Pain from 7 to 3 over 6 visits".
- A table of every visit: **Date**, **Visit**, **Pain**, **Adjusted** and **Scored by** (**Patient, on arrival** or **Practitioner**).
- Below, the **Posture** section with its assessments. See [Posture screen](/articles/clinical/posture-screen).

If there are no scores yet, you'll see "No pain scores yet. They are tapped beside the visit note on My Day, or given by the patient when they check in."

## The progress report

Click **Progress report** to download a PDF ("Progress report · <name>") with the figures, the pain chart, the table of visits and where their care plan stands. It's the document to hand or send to the patient at their re-exam.

The same report opens from **My Day** when the plan is ending. See [Care plans](/articles/clinical/care-plans).

The **Progress** tab is only visible to people with the **See visit notes** permission.
